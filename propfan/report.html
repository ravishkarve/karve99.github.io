Abstract

hsprop predicts the performance of propellers whose blades are so wide and so numerous that the blade row behaves like a compressor cascade: the propfans of the 1980s and the very-high-solidity propellers proposed for Mach 0.8 transport. A load-adapted lifting line carries blade elements that are compressible, swept rows of thin aerofoils. Six models are new: a Prandtl–Glauert map of the blade lattice, verified against Euler solutions; a spanwise-wavenumber relief of the normal Mach number that bounds strip theory near Mach 1; a shock-loss integral built on the exact normal-shock entropy rise; a leading-edge separation model that separates long-bubble decambering on thin unswept sections from the leading-edge vortex on swept ones; passage choke at low power posed as a complementarity condition and solved inside Newton's method together with the circulation; and transition treated as a random process, whose expected location is a smooth functional of the edge-speed distribution. The model was compared with five measured efficiencies of the NASA SR-3 and SR-2 propfans from take-off (Mach 0.2) to Mach 0.85; the mean absolute error is 1.6 points and the largest 2.7. Every operating point from CP 0.6 to 2.6 at Mach 0.8 and at take-off converges, in 2–10 s each.

1The problem

A propfan blade row at Mach 0.8 is three different machines. At the design point the outer blade runs at helical Mach numbers of 1.0–1.15 behind 30–45° of sweep, its sections only 2–3 % thick and, near the root, a solidity above 1: a transonic swept cascade. At take-off the same sections meet the air 5–12° off their design incidence, the leading-edge suction peaks of 2 % sections cannot be carried, and the flow separates at the nose. At low power the root sections turn to negative incidence in passages narrower than their inlet streamtubes, and choke. A blade-element method built on isolated aerofoils and a Prandtl–Glauert factor misses all three. The NASA SR series (eight blades, activity factor 203–235, design point Mach 0.8, J 3.06, CP 1.7) is the reference data set for this class of propeller and is used here for validation.

2Model overview

The blade is a lifting line on its quarter-chord line, discretised into 28 cosine-spaced strips. Its trailing vorticity follows a prescribed helicoidal wake whose pitch and contraction adapt to the loading (wake.py:91); images in the spinner close the wake at the hub. Each strip is evaluated as a blade element in the plane normal to its effective sweep (section.py:273): an infinite row of thin aerofoils with thickness and camber, in compressible flow, with a boundary layer, shock losses, separation and passage choke. The circulation and the choke spillage of all strips are found together by Newton's method (solver.py:381), and the collective pitch is trimmed to a power or thrust coefficient (solver.py:582). The spinner and nacelle enter through a compressible line-source solution (nacelle.py:22).

3The blade element as a compressible rownew

Linear thin-aerofoil theory for a periodic row is solved exactly: the vorticity and source distributions are Glauert series, the row kernel is the periodic Cauchy kernel less its isolated part,

\[ R(\zeta) = \cot\frac{\pi\zeta}{P} - \frac{P}{\pi\zeta}, \qquad \zeta \in \mathbb{C}, \]

and the boundary condition is projected on the Chebyshev modes (Galerkin), cascade.py:136. The flat-plate row reproduces Weinig's exact lift to four digits. Compressibility enters through a new result. The linearised compressible flow through a row of pitch \(s\) and stagger \(\xi\) is, after the Prandtl–Glauert stretch of the chordwise coordinate, an incompressible flow through a different row:

\[ s' = s\sqrt{\sin^2\xi + \beta^2\cos^2\xi}, \qquad \tan\xi' = \frac{\tan\xi}{\beta}, \qquad u_{\text{comp}} = \frac{u'_{\text{inc}}}{\beta}, \qquad \beta = \sqrt{1 - M_n^2}. \]

A dense row therefore feels compressibility through its geometry as well as through the factor \(1/\beta\): the mapped row is more staggered and, for small stagger, denser. The common practice of dividing the incompressible row result by \(\beta\) ignores this. Table 1 compares both with Euler solutions of the same rows (cascade.py:85); a universal table of the mapped-row solution, interpolated in \(\ln(\sigma' + 0.05)\) and \(\xi'\), makes the section evaluation cheap (cascade.py:394).

4Transonic flow

4.1 Three-dimensional reliefnew

Strip theory makes compressibility singular where the normal Mach number reaches 1, and propfan blades have wide chords next to a free tip: the outer blade is a wing of aspect ratio 1–2. A linear disturbance \(e^{i(k_x x + k_z z)}\) on a planar blade decays away from it at the rate

\[ k_y^2 = (1 - M^2)\,k_x^2 + k_z^2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad M_{\text{eff}}^2 = M^2 - \kappa^2, \qquad \kappa = \frac{k_z}{k_x}, \]

so a disturbance with spanwise structure behaves as a two-dimensional one at a lower Mach number. The sources and vortices of the blade end at the tip (the hub is a mirror), so a strip at span distance \(d\) from the tip carries a quarter-wave spanwise mode against the chordwise half wave: \(\kappa = c_n/(2d + c_n)\) (solver.py:271). The relief applies to the chord-scale field. Near the leading edge the disturbance scale is the distance \(x\) itself, so the local factor is

\[ \beta(x)^2 = \beta^2 + \kappa^2 \min\!\left(\frac{2x}{c}, 1\right)^2 , \]

and the suction peak keeps its full compressibility (section.py:319). Local Mach numbers are always recovered from pressures with the actual Mach number. In the lifting line the relief recovers about half of the gap between strip theory and the compressible finite-wing (Helmbold) lift slope at aspect ratio 2.

4.2 Shock lossesnew

The wave drag of each surface is the integral of the entropy rise of a normal shock at the local Mach number over the supersonic region (section.py:368),

\[ c_{d,w} = K_w \sum_{\text{surfaces}} \int \ln\frac{p_{01}}{p_{02}}\big(M(x)\big)\,dx, \qquad K_w = 6, \]

which equals the cube law \((M-1)^3\) near \(M = 1.15\), where the constant is fixed by Korn's drag-divergence equation, reduces to Lock's fourth-power rise and grows more slowly for strong shocks. As the normal Mach number nears 1 the pocket reaches the trailing edge and its terminal shock turns into the oblique trailing-edge shocks of supersonic flow; the drag is then bounded smoothly by that of the same section at the low-supersonic end of the band, \(c_{d,w} \leftarrow (c_{d,w}^{-4} + c_{d,\text{Ackeret}}^{-4})^{-1/4}\). Above \(M_n = 0.9\) the section blends into linear supersonic theory, which it reaches at 1.15.

5High power: leading-edge separationnew

The suction a nose can carry is limited: by a short laminar bubble at low speed (\(C_p \ge -7\)) and by a shock above a local Mach number of 1.45. With \(\eta\) the ratio of that limit to the linear-theory suction peak, the lost part of the leading-edge thrust is drag, as in Carlson's attainable-thrust method (section.py:335):

\[ c_{d,\text{le}} = (1 - \eta)\,\frac{2\pi A_0^2}{\beta}\sqrt{f}, \]

where \(A_0\) is the leading-edge coefficient of the row solution and \(f\) the attached fraction (the suction of a partly separated section scales with \(\sqrt f\), as in the Beddoes–Leishman chord force). What happens next depends on thickness and sweep, after McCullough & Gault's three types of stall (section.py:437):

  • a thick section (normal-plane \(t/c\) above 9 %) stalls at the nose when its bubble bursts;
  • on a thin, unswept section the burst bubble reattaches as a long bubble that keeps the normal force but grows over the chord and decambers it: \(f = 1 - 0.9\,S\big((|A_0|/\beta - 0.085)/0.2\big)\), fitted to NACA 64A006 (lift below potential from about 5°, maximum 0.8 near 9.5°);
  • on a swept edge the separated shear layer rolls up into a leading-edge vortex that is carried along the edge and holds the normal force until it bursts; Polhamus' suction analogy turns the lost suction into vortex lift, \(\Delta c_n = \operatorname{sign}(A_0)\,k_v\,c_{d,\text{le}}\), with \(k_v\) rising from 0 at 10° of sweep to 1 at 40° (section.py:468). Vaczy & McCormick observed such a vortex on propfan blades at take-off.

6Low power: passage choke and spillagenew

A dense row passes at most the mass flow of a sonic throat. With \(\Phi\) the mass-flux function, \(A_1\) the inlet streamtube of one passage and \(o\) the throat between the suction surface of one blade and the pressure surface of the next (section.py:539), the choke ratio is

\[ \chi = \frac{\Phi(M_1)\,A_1}{\Phi^*\,o} \le 1 . \]

A two-dimensional row whose upstream state demands \(\chi > 1\) has no solution; on a propeller the excess flow spills radially round the strip. The spilled inflow \(s\) (fraction of \(V\)) of every strip is therefore a complementarity variable: either \(s = 0\) and \(\chi < 1\), or \(\chi = 1\) and \(s > 0\). It is written with the smoothed Fischer–Burmeister function and solved inside Newton's method together with the circulation:

\[ R_\Gamma = \Gamma - G\big(u_a(\Gamma) - Vs,\; u_t(\Gamma)\big) = 0, \qquad R_s = s + (1 - \chi) - \sqrt{s^2 + (1-\chi)^2 + 2\varepsilon^2} = 0 . \]

Both residuals depend on the strips only through local section functions, so the Jacobian blocks are the influence matrices scaled by section derivatives that two extra sweeps of the section model provide. The blockage that spills the flow raises the pressure ahead of the root; its interaction gain is charged back as spillage drag. Replacing an earlier fixed-point treatment by this formulation cut a low-power solution from 183 s, unconverged, to 9 s with quadratic convergence. Figure 2 shows the result at CP 0.6.

7Boundary layer and transitionnew

The layer is an integral one: Thwaites' method while laminar, Head's entrainment method when turbulent, both with compressibility, and Squire–Young's formula for the drag (boundary_layer.py:85). A first-crossing transition criterion jumps when a second suction peak appears or a bubble forms, and every jump breaks Newton's method on the lifting line. Transition is therefore treated as a random process, as in Emmons' theory of turbulent spots and the intermittency of Dhawan & Narasimha. Spots are born at a rate that grows with the excess over Michel's criterion and with the depth of laminar separation; the probability that the layer is still laminar at \(x\) is \(e^{-B(x)}\), and the transition point is its expectation (boundary_layer.py:140):

\[ B(x) = \int_0^x \left[\frac{(Re_\theta/Re_{\theta,\text{Michel}} - 1)^+}{0.003} + \frac{(\lambda_{\text{on}} - \lambda)^+}{0.09 \times 0.002}\right] dx', \qquad x_{tr} = \int_0^{x_f} e^{-B(x)}\,dx . \]

It is a smooth functional of the edge speeds (Figure 1b). The default trip \(x_f = 0.05\) represents the crossflow, rotation and roughness of real propfan blades; free transition is an option.

8Lifting line, spinner and bookkeeping

The induced velocities come from Biot–Savart integration over the bound and trailing vortices of all blades and their hub images (solver.py:184). On a swept lifting line, thin trailing filaments induce a spurious near-field velocity that depends on the panel width; giving each filament a core of a quarter of the local chord, the scale over which a section sheds its vorticity, removes it, and results do not change for cores between 0.1 and 0.4 chords. The relative state of every strip follows from the rothalpy. Power, thrust and the losses balance exactly (solver.py:481):

\[ P - T V = L_{\text{induced, axial}} + L_{\text{induced, swirl}} + L_{\text{profile}} + L_{\text{wave}} + L_{\text{separation}} + L_{\text{choke}} + L_{\text{spill}} . \]

The work the blades do against the spinner's velocity field returns as the pressure force the propeller field exerts on the body: a potential flow does no net work. The net thrust includes that reaction (solver.py:531), so a spinner earns only its real benefit, a lower Mach number at the blade root. At the SR-3 design point the induced losses are 14.1 % of the power (10.2 % of it swirl), profile drag 5.8 %, shocks 2.5 % and choke 0.3 % (Figure 3).

9Numerics

Newton's method with a backtracking line search and a trust region of 6° on the change of incidence solves for circulation and spillage. The influence matrices are frozen within a Newton solve and the wake is re-shaped a few times. A first solve with attached flow everywhere (a homotopy) starts the full model next to the physical branch. Near stall a propeller has two solutions at one power, attached and stalled at a higher pitch, so the pitch is approached from the attached side, as a governor does: a pitch below the target on the attached branch, from an actuator-disk estimate (solver.py:571), then a bracket and Illinois regula falsi. Every model in §3–7 is a smooth function of the inflow, which keeps the convergence quadratic; a point takes 2–10 s.

10Inverse design

The reference propellers are designed the way the SR blades were (design.py:54). The loading is the Betz optimum in the closed form of Betz and Prandtl, \(B\Gamma = 2\pi r F w \sin\phi\cos\phi\), with Prandtl's tip function \(F\) and no hub loss behind the spinner (design.py:28); the outer 10 % is unloaded for compressibility. Every section carries its share at ideal incidence, zero leading-edge singularity in its compressible swept row, which fixes camber and twist (design.py:42); \(w\) is trimmed to the design power. Designed this way without the 3-D relief, the SR-3-class blade gets an integrated design lift coefficient of 0.196 against the published 0.214.

12Validation against the NASA tests

Neither blade's tabulated geometry was available, so both were reconstructed from their published descriptions (design.py:54): eight blades, 0.622 m diameter, hub-to-tip ratio 0.235 and NACA 16 sections. SR-3 has activity factor 235, 45° tip sweep and an area-ruled spinner; SR-2 is straight, with activity factor 203 and a conventional spinner. Planform and thickness are smooth curves with the published integral properties; camber and twist come from §10. Cruise points are run at the Reynolds number of the NASA Lewis 8×6 ft tunnel, take-off at sea level. tools/validate.py reproduces every number below in about two minutes on four cores.

13What each model contributes

Switching each model off in turn at the SR-3 design point and at take-off (Figure 7) shows where it matters. At take-off the limited leading-edge suction is worth 5.6 points: without it the thin blades would carry suction peaks no real nose can. At cruise the 3-D relief, the transition model, the cascade, sweep and choke spillage each move the efficiency by 1–2 points.

14Limitations

  • The reconstructed geometries are the largest single uncertainty. The model does not resolve the measured 3–5 point advantage of SR-3 over SR-2; it places both within ±3 points of their data.
  • The 3-D relief, Küchemann unsweeping and vortex lift are strip-wise models of three-dimensional effects, not a lifting-surface or CFD solution. Normal Mach numbers above 1.15 use linear supersonic theory.
  • Steady axisymmetric inflow, single rotation; no angle of attack, no contra-rotation, no noise.
  • The net efficiency includes the body's reaction to the propeller field; blade-only efficiency is reported as eta_blade.
